## The practical problem

Agents rarely need memory as isolated fact lookup only. They need to connect facts:

> "Sarah's birthday is March 15."
>
> "Sarah loves Italian food."
>
> "Can you suggest a birthday plan?"

The answer needs both facts. The second fact may not look similar to "birthday plan," but it is still necessary context.

## Why ordinary retrieval misses it

Standard RAG retrieves each chunk independently. Each chunk has to score well against the query on its own.

Multi-hop questions often need a fact that is related to another fact, not directly to the query. This is where association graphs, broader recall, and reranking become useful.

## The benchmark lens

Use multi-hop scores when your product needs cross-session synthesis: personal assistants, project memory, longitudinal coaching, support history, or evolving user profiles.

Use other benchmarks when the product needs something else: temporal updates, abstention, exact quote recovery, policy memory, or controlled long-term retention.
